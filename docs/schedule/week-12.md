This week Andrea will be leading the discussion of Messier et al 2010 [How do 
traits vary across ecological scales? A case for trait-based 
ecology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2010.01476.x)

## Core Questions

- How do environment and geography influence traits at the species and
community level?
- How similar are communities in trait-space?
- How might ecological processes like environmental filtering or interspecific
competition shape community trait distributions?
- Are community traits more clustered or spread out than expected from the
regional species pool? (This might tell us something about process).

## Concepts

- Species traits: body size, age of maturity and reproductive output
- Community mean traits and trait variation within communities
- Working in multivariate trait-space
- Functional dispersion (FDis): mean distance to the community trait centroid
- Ecological processes: Environmental filtering and interspecific competition
- Evaluating significance: Trait randomization and standardized effect size (SES)
- Correlations between traits, latitude, climate and habitat

## Applied Lab

Use prepared Maine amphibian communities in 30 km grid cells and five traits
describing size, maturity, and reproduction. Map community mean traits and
calculate functional dispersion. Compare communities with the same species
richness but different amounts of trait variation.

Randomize trait profiles to compare observed FDis with the variation expected
from the regional species pool. Use Pearson correlations to ask how trait
dispersion relates to latitude, climate, and habitat. Put it all together with a
PCA of community mean traits: map communities with similar life-history traits
and show how environmental predictors relate to their positions in trait-space.
